# TV-anime visual system

Use this guide only when the selected medium is `tv`, or as the TV side of an explicit comparison. Ground exact character colors and episode-specific lighting in inspected files from `TV-photos`; treat this written guide as a baseline when no exact screenshot exists.

## Shape and line

- Preserve the official setting-sheet silhouette, face markers, costume layers, weapon proportions, and relative scale.
- Use clean, economical animation linework with stable contour width. Slightly strengthen the outer silhouette and major overlap lines; keep facial features and small folds lighter.
- Simplify hair into a few large spike or lock groups. Avoid manga-only scratch hatching, broken dry-brush contours, and dense interior strands.
- Keep facial construction readable at animation distance: decisive upper eyelids, compact nose mark, simple mouth, and limited crease lines.

## Color and cel shading

- Derive canonical on-screen colors from the selected TV screenshot whenever available. Do not invent a new palette from manga grayscale values.
- Use flat local colors plus one hard-edged shadow family. Add a second shadow only for a dramatic night or effects shot supported by a TV reference.
- Keep skin gradients minimal or absent. Do not use glossy 3D skin, painterly blending, subsurface scattering, or cinematic color grading.
- Reserve highlights for hair edges, eyes, metal, energy, water, or a clearly motivated light source. Avoid universal rim light and bloom.
- Keep color separation strong enough for the character silhouette to read at thumbnail size.

## Background and lighting

- Use painted-animation backgrounds with simplified shapes, controlled atmospheric depth, and softer detail than the characters.
- Let background values support the foreground silhouette; do not give every leaf, roof tile, stone, and cloud equal sharpness.
- Match day, dusk, night, firelight, mist, or supernatural color effects to the inspected episode reference when exact fidelity is requested.
- Use soft gradients in skies, mist, or distant scenery only. Keep character shading predominantly flat and hard-edged.

## Motion and effects

- Compose action around one readable body/weapon diagonal and a clear attack path.
- Use animation-style speed streaks, impact flashes, dust, aura, and energy shapes without importing printed halftone or manga panel borders.
- Keep effects from washing out identity-critical ears, hair, hands, weapons, jewelry, and costume edges.
- For an episode-shot recreation, preserve camera height, crop, pose, and screen direction before adding new spectacle.

## TV-mode prompt anchors

State observable construction rather than only saying `anime style`:

```text
Color TV-anime frame with clean economical contours, flat canonical local colors, one hard-edged cel-shadow family, restrained highlights, simplified painted background, readable character silhouette, and no manga halftone or printed-ink texture.
```

## Failure patterns

- A colorized manga panel with dot screens, dry-brush ink, or printed paper texture.
- Modern glossy game key art, 3D rendering, photoreal materials, or cinematic bloom.
- Soft airbrush shading across faces and garments.
- Unverified costume or hair colors that conflict with the selected TV screenshot.
- Background detail sharper or denser than the focal characters.
- Manga panel borders, speech balloons, or monochrome speed-line fields in a standalone TV frame.
